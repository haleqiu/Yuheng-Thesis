As I write the final pages of this dissertation, in an era changing rapidly around all of us, I find myself holding a strange mixture of excitement and fear. \texttt{Uncertainty}, the title of this thesis, has also been the rhythm of my research and of these past few years. In state estimation, both overconfidence and underconfidence can lead to failure; in life, I have found the same to be true. My fears, too, were born from uncertainty: the uncertainty of taking a risk, of making a mistake, of leaving the familiar behind, and of beginning again.

What carried me through was never the private resolution of these uncertainties. It was you — my mentors, my friends, my fellow travelers, my family, and my love. Your support and encouragement made each step possible.
I am only an ordinary explorer in a complex world. But with you beside me, I have found the courage to keep walking, and perhaps even to make some small difference.

\begin{center}
\textbf{You are my certainty in this uncertain world.}
\end{center}

\ackheading{The Mentor Who Shaped the Journey.}
A doctorate is, before anything else, a chain of mentors who chose to take a chance on me, and I owe a great deal to mine.

To my advisor, Sebastian Scherer, I owe both this thesis and the way I now think about research.
I still remember the first time we met, when you told me, “Yuheng, following a best paper will not give you another best paper.” The greatest lesson I have taken from you is that the fact that others are doing something is never, by itself, a reason for us to do the same. You taught me the value of independent thinking, and the discipline of reasoning from first principles. This, more than any single result, is the greatest fortune I will carry with me into whatever journey comes after this thesis.

Wenshan has been a second mentor to me since my earliest days in the AirLab. Wenshan, at a time when I was still finding my footing in a new environment, you helped me settle in and feel at home much faster than I could have on my own. Since then, you have generously shared your wisdom, encouragement, and perspective, both in research and in life.
I am deeply grateful for your mentorship, your trust, and the steady support you have given me throughout these years.

I am also deeply grateful to Chen Wang, who shaped my research interests and taste at a very early stage of my PhD journey. Chen, many of my earliest ideas about what makes a research problem meaningful, elegant, and worth pursuing were influenced by conversations with you. You helped me see research not only as solving technical problems, but also as developing a sense of direction.

Junyi Geng, I am deeply grateful for your mathematical insight and the many suggestions you have offered me over the years. Your comments were always sharp, precise, and direct to the heart of the problem. In moments when I was lost in technical details, you often helped me see the essential structure more clearly. I still remember something you once said: every student is like a young tree, and mentorship is about understanding how to prune and shape it. That sentence has stayed with me

In the final stretch of this Ph.D., I had the privilege of being mentored at Amazon FAR by an extraordinary group: Professor Guanya Shi, Rocky Duan, Professor Pieter Abbeel, Professor Karen Liu, and Carmelo Sferrazza.
Coming from different research traditions, each of you taught me to think more deeply from first principles about what it means to solve robotics. Your perspectives helped me look beyond individual algorithms and see uncertainty-aware perception as part of a broader question: how learning, control, and embodied intelligence can work together at scale.
These conversations sharpened the framing of the final chapters of this thesis and gave me a clearer sense of where this line of research can go after the defense. I am deeply grateful for your guidance, insight, and encouragement during this important stage of my Ph.D.
