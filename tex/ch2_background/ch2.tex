\subsection{Evolution from Sensor Fusion to Uncertainty Learning}

Traditional robotic perception systems have relied on hand-tuned uncertainty models that assume fixed noise characteristics across different environments and platforms. Early probabilistic frameworks like the Extended Kalman Filter (EKF)\cite{thrun2005probabilistic} and Unscented Kalman Filter (UKF)\cite{julier2004unscented} use manually specified covariance matrices for sensor measurements. While mathematically principled, these approaches struggle to capture the complex, environment-dependent nature of sensor noise, leading to suboptimal fusion performance and poor generalization across different operational conditions.

The recognition of this limitation has driven the evolution toward learning-based uncertainty estimation. Modern tightly coupled systems like VINS-Fusion~\cite{qin2018vins} and OKVIS~\cite{leutenegger2015keyframe} still rely on factor graph optimization~\cite{dellaert2017factor}, but the fundamental challenge remains: how to accurately model uncertainty without extensive manual parameter tuning for each new environment or sensor configuration.

Recognizing the scalability and computational bottlenecks of tightly coupled methods, hybrid approaches emerged. A notable example is Super Odometry, which proposes an IMU-centric sensor fusion framework. By prioritizing the IMU as the primary estimator and selectively incorporating other sensor modalities, hybrid methods balance robustness, computational efficiency, and system simplicity—offering a more scalable solution compared to fully tightly coupled frameworks.

This gap has motivated the development of learning-based uncertainty estimation frameworks. Instead of relying on fixed covariance matrices, emerging approaches like VINet~\cite{clark2017vinet} and DynaSLAM~\cite{bescos2018dynaslam} explore data-driven uncertainty models. However, most existing learning-based methods focus on end-to-end state estimation rather than principled uncertainty modeling, limiting their theoretical foundations and practical deployment in safety-critical applications.

The emergence of foundation models represents a paradigm shift toward generalizable uncertainty estimation. Works like Dust3R~\cite{wang2024dust3r} demonstrate that pre-trained models can provide robust uncertainty priors across diverse environments. This thesis extends this concept by developing specialized uncertainty learning frameworks for individual sensor modalities (visual, inertial) and their fusion, enabling truly generalizable spatial intelligence that adapts to new platforms and environments without manual recalibration.

Despite substantial progress, achieving robust, real-time, and scalable SLAM across diverse operational domains remains an active area of research.

\subsection{Uncertainty-Aware Spatial Intelligence}
The key challenge in developing truly intelligent robotic systems lies not just in sensor fusion, but in understanding and modeling the inherent uncertainty in perception. Real-world robotics applications—from underground exploration to contact-rich manipulation—expose sensors to conditions that deviate significantly from laboratory settings, making fixed uncertainty models inadequate.

Traditional approaches treat uncertainty as a nuisance to be minimized rather than valuable information to be leveraged. However, uncertainty quantification is fundamental to spatial intelligence: knowing what the robot doesn't know enables safer decision-making, more robust planning, and adaptive behavior in uncertain environments. This paradigm shift from uncertainty avoidance to uncertainty awareness represents a critical evolution in robotic perception.

This thesis develops a comprehensive framework for learning-based uncertainty estimation across the entire robotic perception pipeline. Starting with individual sensor modalities (Chapter~\ref{chapter:macvo} for visual uncertainty, Chapter~\ref{chapter:airimu} for inertial uncertainty), progressing through multi-sensor fusion (Chapters~\ref{chapter:macvio} and \ref{chapter:macio}), and extending to physical interaction scenarios (Chapters~\ref{chapter:contact_learning} and \ref{chapter:perceptive_locomotion}), we demonstrate that learned uncertainty models enable truly generalizable spatial intelligence.








\section{Problem Definition}
\label{sec:problem_definition}
The goal of a sensor fusion SLAM (Simultaneous Localization and Mapping) system is to estimate the trajectory of a mobile robot and construct a consistent map of the surrounding environment by fusing complementary data from multiple heterogeneous sensors. These sensors typically include, but are not limited to, inertial measurement units (IMUs), cameras (monocular, stereo, or RGB-D), LiDAR, and thermal or radar sensors.

Formally, let $\mathcal{X}_{1:T} = \{ \mathbf{x}_1, \mathbf{x}_2, \dots, \mathbf{x}_T \}$ denote the sequence of robot poses over time, and let $\mathcal{M}$ denote the map of the environment. The robot receives a sequence of multi-modal sensor measurements $\mathcal{Z}_{1:T} = \{ \mathbf{z}_1, \mathbf{z}_2, \dots, \mathbf{z}_T \}$, where each $\mathbf{z}_t$ may consist of visual, inertial, LiDAR, or other sensor observations at time $t$.

The core problem is to estimate the posterior:
\begin{equation}
p(\mathcal{X}_{1:T}, \mathcal{M} \mid \mathcal{Z}_{1:T}),
\end{equation}
which is generally intractable and approximated through filtering, smoothing, or optimization techniques.

A sensor fusion SLAM system must address the following challenges:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Heterogeneous Sensor Characteristics}: Each sensor modality has different noise models, failure modes, and operating conditions, requiring careful calibration and uncertainty modeling.
    \item \textbf{Asynchronous and Multi-rate Data}: Sensors often operate at different frequencies and latencies, demanding accurate time synchronization and robust interpolation strategies.
    \item \textbf{Degenerate and Degraded Conditions}: The system must remain operational in environments where certain modalities fail, such as low-light for cameras or featureless regions for LiDAR.
    \item \textbf{Real-time Constraints}: To be practical for autonomous systems, sensor fusion and state estimation must be performed efficiently and within the constraints of onboard compute resources.
\end{itemize}

The objective is to design a SLAM framework that fuses multi-modal sensory information in a resilient and adaptive manner, enabling long-term, accurate localization and mapping in complex, dynamic, and degraded environments.
