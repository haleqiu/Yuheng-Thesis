\chapter{Introduction} \label{chapter:introduction}
\fancyhead[LO]{\makebox[\textwidth][r]{Chapter 1. Introduction}}

The field of robotics faces a fundamental challenge: while we have sophisticated sensors and algorithms, current perception systems fail to generalize across diverse environments and platforms due to their reliance on hand-tuned uncertainty models. 
Traditional robotic perception approaches use fixed covariance matrices and empirically-set noise parameters that work well in controlled scenarios but break down when deployed in new environments, on different platforms, or under varying operational conditions.

This brittleness stems from a deeper problem: the inability to accurately model and propagate uncertainty through the perception pipeline. Current systems treat uncertainty as an afterthought, using simplistic models that fail to capture the true reliability of sensor measurements and their correlations across modalities.

My research addresses this fundamental limitation through a comprehensive framework for learning-based uncertainty estimation. Rather than relying on hand-crafted parameters, this dissertation demonstrates how data-driven uncertainty models can be learned from experience, adapted across platforms, and propagated through complex perception pipelines to enable truly generalizable robotic perception systems.

\section{Problem Statement: The Need for Generalizable Perception}
\label{sec:problem_statement}

Traditional uncertainty models fail across multiple scenarios that are common in real-world robotics deployments. 
For instance, stereo cameras calibrated in indoor laboratory conditions produce overconfident uncertainty estimates when deployed in outdoor environments with varying lighting, leading to poor localization when integrated with other sensors. Similarly, IMU noise models tuned for one robotic platform (e.g., a ground vehicle) perform poorly when transferred to aerial platforms due to different vibration characteristics and mounting configurations.

Current robotic perception systems exhibit three fundamental limitations:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Hand-Tuned Uncertainty Models}: Current perception systems rely on manually specified covariance matrices and noise parameters that assume stationary, Gaussian noise characteristics. These fixed models cannot capture the complex, environment-dependent variations in sensor reliability, leading to either overconfident or overly conservative estimates.
    \item \textbf{Platform-Specific Calibration}: Each new robotic platform requires extensive manual parameter tuning by domain experts, involving weeks of data collection and empirical validation. This process does not scale to the diversity of robots and environments needed for widespread autonomous system deployment.
    \item \textbf{Poor Cross-Modal Integration}: Existing multi-sensor fusion approaches treat uncertainties from different modalities as independent, ignoring the complex correlations that exist between visual, inertial, and other sensory measurements. This leads to suboptimal fusion weights and missed opportunities for uncertainty-driven sensor selection.
\end{enumerate}

This dissertation addresses these limitations through a comprehensive learning-based uncertainty estimation framework that replaces hand-tuned models with data-driven approaches. By learning uncertainty models directly from sensor data across diverse conditions and platforms, we enable robotic perception systems that automatically adapt their reliability estimates, generalize across environments, and optimally fuse multi-modal information based on learned uncertainty correlations.

\section{Research Challenges in Learning-Based Robotic Perception}
\label{sec:challenges}

This dissertation tackles the following key challenges:

\subsection{Challenge 1: Uncertainty Modeling for Individual Sensors}
Learning reliable uncertainty models for individual sensors presents several technical challenges: capturing non-stationary noise characteristics that vary with environmental conditions, ensuring uncertainty estimates remain well-calibrated across diverse operational scenarios, and developing computationally efficient models that can run in real-time on robotic platforms.

\subsection{Challenge 2: Cross-Modal Uncertainty Fusion}
Multi-sensor uncertainty fusion is challenging because different modalities exhibit complex temporal and spatial correlations that traditional independence assumptions fail to capture. Learning these correlations requires understanding how failures in one sensor affect others, and developing adaptive fusion strategies that can dynamically weight sensor contributions based on learned uncertainty relationships.

\subsection{Challenge 3: Uncertainty-Aware Physical Interaction}
Contact-rich robotics presents unique challenges for uncertainty propagation because perception errors directly affect physical safety and task success. Uncertainty must be propagated through complex kinodynamic models to inform contact decisions, requiring new theoretical frameworks that connect perception uncertainty to safe exploration strategies and adaptive control policies.

\section{Impact on Autonomous Robotics}
\label{sec:impact}

The learning-based uncertainty estimation framework developed in this thesis has the potential to transform how robotic systems are deployed and operate in real-world environments. By eliminating the need for manual uncertainty parameter tuning, this work significantly reduces the expertise and time required to deploy robotic systems across new platforms and environments. This democratization of robust perception enables broader adoption of autonomous systems across industries, from manufacturing to exploration.

This framework enables several transformative capabilities:

\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Plug-and-Play Deployment}: Learned uncertainty models automatically adapt to new robotic platforms and sensor configurations, enabling zero-shot deployment without requiring extensive parameter tuning or domain expertise.
    \item \textbf{Adaptive Reliability}: Uncertainty estimates dynamically adjust to changing environmental conditions, providing accurate reliability indicators that enable robust decision-making in diverse operational scenarios.
    \item \textbf{Contact-Rich Applications}: Uncertainty propagation from perception to contact enables safer physical interaction strategies, opening new possibilities for manipulation and locomotion in unstructured environments.
    \item \textbf{Safety and Robustness}: Principled uncertainty quantification enables conservative behavior when confidence is low and aggressive performance when conditions are favorable, improving both safety and efficiency.
\end{itemize}
